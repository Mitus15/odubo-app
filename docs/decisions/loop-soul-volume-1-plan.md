## Kamloops marketing plan

**The engine: the owner's own performances.** Record dancing/performing to his
own music through the Loop Soul filter → Instagram/TikTok → "get a pass" link.
This proves the filter, the vibe and the artist in one post, and it's content
only Loop Soul can make. Cadence: 3×/week from announcement to the event.

**Channels**
- **Instagram/TikTok** — the performance series; Danceyokey teasers ("this is
  your spot"); the anthem ("what should we all dance to?"); poster reveals.
- **Campus (TRU)** — back-to-school barbecue and first-weeks activity; posters
  and QR codes where students actually stand; a student-priced push if desired.
- **Local venues/cafés** — posters with the QR (the QR is on every poster from
  Poster Studio).
- **Affiliates/promoters** — brief #5 gives them the concept and a reason to
  bring their people; consider a per-promoter code batch so reach is measurable.
- **Scott's Inn's own audience** — the venue's guests and channels (brief #4).

**Message hierarchy:** it's not a night out, it's *a taping of a series about
Kamloops soul* — you're in the room, and you might be in the episode.

## Product work this implies (queued, not built)

### 1. The digital pass (high priority — buyers get it immediately)
What a buyer receives after checkout, and what makes the purchase feel real:
- A designed pass, not just a code in an email.
- **Scannable QR** on it (same link as the posters).
- **Printable**, **savable to Photos as a PNG**, **keepable in a wallet**.
- Distinct from the paper ticket/wristband: this is the *personal* artifact.

### 2. Selfie-to-start-your-gallery (onboarding, in-room)
To start a gallery, a guest takes **a selfie first**. That selfie is run
through the Loop Soul filter and becomes **the cover of their gallery**. After
that they shoot freely, with or without the filter. Ties identity, attribution
and the filter into one step that feels like a ritual rather than a form.

### 3. Progressive familiarity (not overwhelm)
Information about Loop Soul should reach people in layers, so that **by the
time someone holds a pass they already know how the app works**. Practically:
the pre-code preview (queued), the explainer videos, the tutorials, and a
first-run walkthrough in the app itself.

### 4. Legal compliance throughout
Photo/video consent at capture (the room is being filmed for a series), the
terms attached to contributor royalties, privacy for attendee identity and
email, and age/venue rules. Needs a proper pass before any public launch —
flag for review, not something to improvise.

### 5. Vectorize the silhouettes — ASAP, and it becomes a pipeline
Not just for scaling the current art: **guests photographed at an event should
be turnable into poster figures for future volumes**. That makes the poster kit
self-feeding — every night produces next season's artwork. Requires a
raster→vector step on top of the existing capture pipeline.
